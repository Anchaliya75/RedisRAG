Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen using chlorophyll in their leaves. The process occurs in two stages: light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid membranes and light-independent reactions in the stroma. During the light reactions, water molecules are split to release oxygen, and energy is captured in ATP and NADPH. In the Calvin cycle, carbon dioxide is fixed into organic molecules using the energy from ATP and NADPH.

DNA replication is the biological process of producing two identical copies of DNA from one original molecule. The process begins at specific locations called origins of replication, where the double helix is unwound by helicase enzymes. DNA polymerase then synthesizes new strands by adding complementary nucleotides to each template strand. The leading strand is synthesized continuously, while the lagging strand is made in short Okazaki fragments that are later joined by DNA ligase.

Evolution by natural selection is the process where organisms with favorable traits are more likely to survive and reproduce. Charles Darwin proposed this theory after observing variation among species during his voyage on HMS Beagle. Natural selection acts on phenotypic variation within populations, and over many generations, advantageous traits become more common. This process, combined with genetic drift, mutation, and gene flow, drives the diversity of life on Earth.

Cells are the basic structural and functional units of all living organisms. Eukaryotic cells contain membrane-bound organelles including the nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and Golgi apparatus. The nucleus houses the cell's genetic material and controls gene expression. Mitochondria generate ATP through oxidative phosphorylation, earning them the title of cellular powerhouses.

The immune system is a complex network that defends the body against pathogens including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites. Innate immunity provides immediate, non-specific defense through physical barriers, phagocytes, and inflammation. Adaptive immunity develops over time and provides targeted responses through T cells and B cells. B cells produce antibodies that bind to specific antigens, while T cells can directly kill infected cells or help coordinate the immune response.

Mitosis is the process of cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells from a single parent cell. The process proceeds through four main phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase. During prophase, chromosomes condense and the nuclear envelope breaks down. In metaphase, chromosomes align at the cell's equator, and during anaphase, sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles by spindle fibers.

Protein synthesis is the process by which cells build proteins according to genetic instructions. Transcription occurs in the nucleus, where RNA polymerase reads a DNA template to produce messenger RNA. The mRNA then travels to ribosomes in the cytoplasm, where translation occurs. Transfer RNA molecules bring amino acids to the ribosome, matching their anticodons to mRNA codons to build polypeptide chains.

Ecosystems are communities of living organisms interacting with their physical environment. Energy flows through ecosystems from producers to consumers through food chains and food webs. Nutrient cycling involves the movement of elements like carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus through biotic and abiotic components. Biodiversity within ecosystems provides resilience against environmental changes and supports ecosystem services essential for human survival.
