Chemical bonding occurs when atoms interact to form stable arrangements by sharing or transferring electrons. Covalent bonds involve the sharing of electron pairs between atoms, forming molecules like water and methane. Ionic bonds result from the transfer of electrons from metals to nonmetals, creating charged ions held together by electrostatic forces. Metallic bonds involve a sea of delocalized electrons shared among metal atoms, giving metals their characteristic conductivity and malleability.

The periodic table organizes all known chemical elements by increasing atomic number and recurring chemical properties. Elements in the same group share similar electron configurations and chemical behavior, while periods show gradual changes in properties. Trends across the table include increasing electronegativity from left to right and decreasing atomic radius. The table's structure reflects quantum mechanical principles of electron shell filling and predicts element properties.

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon-containing compounds and their properties, structures, and reactions. Carbon's ability to form four covalent bonds and chain together enables an enormous diversity of molecular structures. Key functional groups include hydroxyl, carboxyl, amino, and carbonyl groups that determine chemical reactivity. Organic chemistry is fundamental to biochemistry, pharmaceuticals, materials science, and the petrochemical industry.

Electrochemistry studies the relationship between electrical energy and chemical reactions. Galvanic cells convert chemical energy to electrical energy through spontaneous redox reactions, as seen in batteries. Electrolytic cells use electrical energy to drive non-spontaneous reactions, enabling processes like electroplating and water splitting. Understanding electrode potentials, the Nernst equation, and Faraday's laws is essential for developing better batteries, fuel cells, and corrosion prevention methods.
