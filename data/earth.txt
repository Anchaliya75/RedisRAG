Plate tectonics is the theory that Earth's outer shell is divided into several large plates that float on the semi-fluid asthenosphere. These plates move due to convection currents in the mantle, causing them to collide, separate, or slide past each other at boundaries. Convergent boundaries create mountains and subduction zones, divergent boundaries form mid-ocean ridges, and transform boundaries cause earthquakes like along the San Andreas Fault. This theory explains the distribution of earthquakes, volcanoes, and the formation of continents over geological time.

Climate change refers to significant long-term changes in global temperatures and weather patterns driven by human activities. The burning of fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. Global average temperatures have risen approximately 1.1 degrees Celsius since pre-industrial times, causing melting ice caps, rising sea levels, and more frequent extreme weather events. International efforts like the Paris Agreement aim to limit warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius through emissions reductions and renewable energy adoption.

Oceanography is the scientific study of all aspects of the ocean, covering its physics, chemistry, biology, and geology. Ocean currents driven by wind, temperature, and salinity differences circulate heat around the globe, regulating climate patterns. The deep ocean contains hydrothermal vents that support unique ecosystems independent of sunlight through chemosynthesis. Understanding ocean acidification, caused by absorption of excess atmospheric carbon dioxide, is critical for predicting impacts on marine ecosystems and coral reefs.
