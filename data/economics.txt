Microeconomics analyzes how individuals, households, and firms make decisions about allocating scarce resources. The interaction of supply and demand determines market prices and quantities in competitive markets. Key concepts include price elasticity, consumer surplus, producer surplus, and market equilibrium. Market failures such as externalities, public goods, and information asymmetry justify government intervention through taxation, regulation, and subsidies.

Macroeconomics examines the economy as a whole, focusing on aggregate indicators like gross domestic product, inflation, and unemployment. Central banks use monetary policy tools including interest rates and money supply to influence economic activity and control inflation. Fiscal policy involves government spending and taxation decisions to stimulate or cool down the economy during business cycles. Understanding the interplay between monetary and fiscal policy is essential for maintaining economic stability and promoting sustainable growth.
