Calculus is the mathematical study of continuous change, divided into differential and integral calculus. Differential calculus deals with derivatives, which measure instantaneous rates of change and slopes of curves. Integral calculus computes areas under curves, volumes of solids, and accumulated quantities through antiderivatives. Together, they are connected by the fundamental theorem of calculus and form the foundation for physics, engineering, and economics.

Linear algebra studies vector spaces, matrices, linear transformations, and systems of linear equations. Matrices represent transformations, and operations like multiplication, inversion, and decomposition are central tools. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors reveal fundamental properties of linear transformations and appear in principal component analysis, Google's PageRank, and quantum mechanics. The field is essential for machine learning, computer graphics, signal processing, and optimization.

Probability theory provides a mathematical framework for quantifying uncertainty and random phenomena. Key concepts include sample spaces, events, conditional probability, and random variables with their distributions. Bayes' theorem relates conditional probabilities and forms the basis for Bayesian inference in statistics and machine learning. The law of large numbers and central limit theorem are foundational results that connect probability to statistics.

Graph theory studies mathematical structures called graphs, consisting of vertices connected by edges. Graphs model relationships in social networks, transportation systems, computer networks, and biological pathways. Key algorithms include shortest path finding, minimum spanning trees, graph coloring, and network flow optimization. Graph databases and knowledge graphs apply these concepts to store and query interconnected data efficiently.

Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting numerical data to draw conclusions. Descriptive statistics summarize data through measures of central tendency and dispersion like mean, median, and standard deviation. Inferential statistics uses hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and regression analysis to make predictions about populations from samples. Modern statistics incorporates computational methods like bootstrapping and Bayesian inference for complex data analysis.

Number theory is the branch of mathematics devoted to studying the properties and relationships of integers. Prime numbers are central to number theory, with the distribution of primes described by the prime number theorem. Modular arithmetic, congruences, and Diophantine equations are key topics with applications in cryptography. The Riemann hypothesis, one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics, concerns the distribution of prime numbers.
