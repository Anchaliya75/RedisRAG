Our solar system formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust called the solar nebula. It contains eight planets divided into rocky inner planets and gas giant outer planets, along with dwarf planets, asteroid belts, and the Oort Cloud. The Sun contains over 99 percent of the solar system's total mass and drives planetary orbits through gravitational attraction. Space missions from Voyager to the James Webb Space Telescope continue to reveal new details about our cosmic neighborhood.

Astronomy is the scientific study of celestial objects, space, and the physical universe as a whole. Observational astronomy uses telescopes across the electromagnetic spectrum from radio to gamma rays to study distant objects. The Big Bang theory describes the origin of the universe approximately 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state. Dark matter and dark energy make up about 95 percent of the universe's total mass-energy content, yet their nature remains one of the biggest mysteries in modern physics.

Einstein's special theory of relativity fundamentally changed our understanding of space, time, and energy. The theory is built on two postulates: the laws of physics are the same in all inertial reference frames, and the speed of light in vacuum is constant for all observers. Time dilation causes moving clocks to tick slower relative to stationary observers, and length contraction shortens objects in the direction of motion. The famous equation E equals mc squared reveals that mass and energy are interchangeable, with a small amount of mass converting to enormous energy.
